    🗺️ Dynamic Routes¶

    Routes grows a road network through your world during worldgen — no commands, no walking the area first. Cities are found from the world seed, connected with terrain-aware paths, and every road is signposted and named.

    What you get¶

    • Automatic road network between cities — vanilla villages, modpack villages, the gym entrances of the packs Routes ships a list for, and any structure you list. Built incrementally and resumed after a restart.
    • Flat & inland paths — the pathfinder strongly penalises slopes and keeps a buffer away from shorelines, while still crossing water when there's no alternative.
    • Mineshaft tunnels — when a climb is too steep (or there's no surface path at all), the route bores a clean, walkable, oak-framed tunnel — twice the road's width and eight blocks tall — instead of an impassable cliff. About a third of tunnels are atmospheric dark caves — bring torches!
    • Descriptive route names — every road gets a code plus an auto-generated name from the terrain it crosses (Route 5: Coastal Road, Route 8: Marsh Crossing), always the same for your seed.
    • Clean tree clearing — a road crossing a tree removes the whole trunk and canopy; no floating leaves, no item spam. Trees and huge mushrooms don't grow within a few blocks of the road's edge, so no crown hangs over the road where you walk. Any leaves or branches a tree hangs into the 3 blocks above a road, including the strip right at its edge, are trimmed away; the forest beside it is left.
    • Water crossings — always on the surface. A river gets a plank bridge with its pillars dropped to the riverbed, in one wood from end to end; a longer stretch stays an open lane of water to swim or sail, marked on both edges by natural rock markers, most of them lit, and the road meets it at the shore's own height. The seabed is never touched, and a frozen lake keeps its ice. Water the planner could not see coming (a river carved later by Streams Reflowing, an aquifer pool) is crossed on a timber bridge at the banks' height, not left as a swim gap. With Streams Reflowing installed, roads keep off its river banks and cross its rivers squarely.
    • Bridges on real supports — the posts under a bridge's edges are full logs of the bridge's own wood, with a log beam across under the deck at every post row.
    • Climbs you can walk — a road that climbs steps up in half blocks (slab, full block, slab), in the road's own material: it starts a climb a few blocks early and finishes it a few blocks late, so up to four rises in a row are walked in slabs. Only a longer climb keeps a stair, where the slabs run out. Every one-block step gets a stair or a slab, on the ramps up to a bridge, into a tunnel and out of a town too; a bridge deck that rises one block takes a slab. A wall that is still two blocks or more gets a ladder, whichever way you walk in. Where a road reaches a bridge ramp half a block low, both blocks are stairs, so there is no wall to jump.
    • Roads made of what you choose — the surface can be one material or a mix laid in rounded patches, with an optional share of worn blocks (cracked or mossy bricks, gravel in cobblestone, grass through a dirt path). Bridge decks and handrails are chosen the same way, one material per bridge and per route. Left on Random, each stretch takes the palette of the biome it crosses. See Configuration.
    • Signs everywhere — directional signs at each end and at junctions (one per road, even at a fork right by a chunk border), with plaza-paved crossings that stand out from the road, and a welcome board with the town's name where a road arrives.
    • Junctions built for the spot — where two roads cross, the crossing is either a ringed roundabout around a lit pillar or an open signed crossing with fingerposts naming all four destinations. Roads that meet at different heights are an overpass: the upper one simply bridges over. A fork that lands inside a hill opens into a chamber where its tunnels meet, with its signposts on the chamber floor beside the roads. On a hillside the ground around a fork is levelled for its signposts, and each road stays level across it and starts to climb at its edge. A road's signpost is not put where another road runs.
    • Rest stops — every few hundred blocks, a small camp just off the road: a campfire with log seats, a lantern post and a sign. Now and then one keeps a supply chest from the last traveller — rarely, so finding one still means something. With Cobblemon Picnic installed, now and then a stop is one of its picnic tables instead — about 3 in 100, set by Picnic stops in Configuration.
    • Landmarks along the way — a road passing a pyramid, a tower or a ruin bends a little to go by it, keeps off its footprint, and puts up a signpost with its name. No road is planned to a landmark — see Which structures get roads.
    • Villages, towns and cities — a settlement's real size decides how many roads leave it, so the network has quiet hamlets and busy hubs. A fork out of a big town stands past its last houses, never among them. A road ends where a village really ends: a leftover scrap of road between two houses is left as the village's own ground. A structure tagged routes:settlement/village, routes:settlement/town or routes:settlement/city gets that word after its name, so Pallet becomes Pallet Town. The cities of Cobblemon Cities & Structures (Pewter, Cerulean, Vermilion, Celadon, Fuchsia, Saffron, Cinnabar and Viridian, plus Lavender and Pallet when placed by hand) are towns of the network too.
    • Underground towns — any structure can be set as an Underground town in Which structures get roads. Its road ends at the surface, and right beside the end a stone-brick spiral stairway goes down to the town: slab half-steps, a landing every 8 blocks down, a short tunnel at the bottom, and a ↓ town board at the top naming the town below. Two underground towns are never joined directly; each is reached from a town on the surface. Nothing is set this way by default.
    • Route Pokémon — with Cobblemon 1.8 installed, roads carry habitat blocks hidden in the paving, each spawning its own Pokémon family at its own level band, leaning toward the biome's natives (Water types by water crossings, Flying types on bridges). No legendaries, Ultra Beasts, Paradox Pokémon or starters. Turn them off with Route Pokémon in Configuration.
    • Route trainers — with Radical Cobblemon Trainers installed, a trainer stands beside the road every 160 blocks as you explore it, with a team near your party's level, and swimmers at water spots. No gym leaders, Elite Four or Champions. Without RCT nothing changes.
    • Roads meet the town's own streets — a road joins a town at the open end of a street, at street level, never behind a house or climbing into the air. A pack can mark its street ends with an open jigsaw named routes:city_exit, and the road then continues the street that faces it most squarely.
    • Spawn in a city — new worlds start next to the nearest city, on real ground, never in the water — coastal towns included.
    • A loading screen that shows the real wait — while a new world opens, two towns and the road between them are the progress bar. Each step (surveying the towns around your spawn, generating the spawn chunks, building your spawn town) gets its share of the road by how long it really takes. The road's tip only moves while work actually finishes, the line under it counts tasks done, and it says no progress for N s when nothing has finished for a while. Until you are in the world the survey uses every processor core but one, and goes back to four once you are playing; the towns and roads of a seed are the same either way.

    Tune it per world

    How many roads, how they look, materials, lighting and more are all set from the ROUTES tab at world creation, with one-click Road network and Road style presets on top — see World Creation & Config.
