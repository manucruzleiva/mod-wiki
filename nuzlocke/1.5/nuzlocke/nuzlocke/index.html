    🎮 Hardcore Nuzlocke¶

    The classic ruleset, enforced by the game instead of by your honour. Every rule below is a switch on the NUZLOCKE tab at world creation, and every one of them can be changed later — see Commands & Config. The one thing that is not a switch is the difficulty: while the ruleset is on, the world is set to Hard every time it starts.

    The rules¶

    Rule What it means
    🎯 One catch per zone Only the first wild you meet in a zone is catchable. A spent zone bounces Poké Balls, in battle and out. Fleeing or knocking the encounter out spends it just the same.
    💀 Permadeath A fainted Pokémon is dead: no healing of any kind, no battling, no trading. The PC or release, nothing else.
    ✍️ Mandatory nicknames A blocking screen on every Pokémon you obtain, showing its sprite, gender, ability and moves — so you name it knowing what it is. Leaving the game with the screen open brings it back on your next login, and you keep the Pokémon. Your starter is the exception: leave before naming it and the pick is undone, so you choose again from the same three.
    ✨ Shiny clause A wild shiny is always catchable and never spends the zone.
    🔁 Duplicates clause A Pokémon whose line you already own cannot be caught, and does not spend the zone — that encounter waits for something new. What you own includes your starter, gifts, trades and everything you have evolved.
    🥚 Egg clause A hatchling is tied to where it hatches, like a wild catch: it takes that zone's encounter, so an egg will not hatch in a zone you have already spent — it waits until you carry it somewhere new. This holds even with the first-encounter lock off, and a shiny hatchling spends the zone like any other. Eggs can also be banned outright, or exempted.
    😴 Defeated Catch A wild you beat lies on the ground for a while, asleep at 1 HP and catchable — a second chance at the encounter, KO experience included. While it lies there it can only be caught, never battled again.
    😠 Aggressive wilds About one species in five comes after you instead of meleeing you, and its first hit starts a battle. A wild above your level cap never does.
    🏥 Whiteout Losing a battle sends you back to your respawn point: the last Pokémon Center you used, or your bed, or the world spawn without one. Running out of usable Pokémon anywhere is a Game Over.
    📈 Level cap Follows your trainer card. Catches above it go straight to the PC, and no wild Pokémon above it turns a hit into a battle.
    🎒 No bag in battle The battle bag is closed. A second mode blocks only healing, leaving X items legal — Poké Balls are never blocked.

    Zones¶

    A zone is a place, and the place decides the rule. What counts as one place is your choice at world creation, under Capture zones are:

    Mode A zone is
    Geography The world Routes draws: every numbered Area, every named route and every town is its own zone. A route's zone reaches one chunk past the road, so a Pokémon caught just off it counts for the route; where that strip touches two routes, the lower route number takes it. Everything not enclosed in an Area is one shared Wild zone, one per dimension — it is in your log from the first minute, and early on it is most of where you can catch, so growing the road network is what earns you new zones beside it.
    Biome The biome you are standing in. This is the classic tabletop rule, and the one that works from the first minute — it does not wait for a road network to exist.
    Both The pair. A route that crosses taiga and then swamp is two zones and gives you two encounters, listed as Route 3 — Taiga and Route 3 — Swamp.

    Under Geography and Both, structures are sites, and a site grants one encounter per KIND of site: your first pillager outpost is an encounter, the next outpost is a place you have already spent. Site encounters can be switched off entirely, in which case a structure counts as whatever surrounds it.

    Changing the mode part-way through a run re-reads every catch you have already made, which can re-open or merge zones you had spent. The setting says so where you pick it.

    Crossing into a new zone puts its name on screen as a title, with its state beside it: ○ free, ● an encounter in progress, ✔ caught, ✘ lost. The title can be switched off.

    Zones a map maker drew¶

    A world builder can mark out their own towns, routes and areas with Routes' zone commands, and the run plays by them: walking into one announces the name they gave it, the one-catch rule covers that whole zone, and the log lists it under that name. Resizing or renaming a zone keeps the catch already made in it. Deleting one hands its ground back to the zones the generator drew.

    The log¶

    Press I (rebindable) for the run's record: every zone you have discovered, what you caught there, and whether it is still alive. Four chips across the top — Alive, Dead, Waiting, Empty — each say how many lines they stand for and turn that group off, so a long run can be read down to just the part you are after. Most of a long log is Empty: zones you have found and not yet had an encounter in. A second row filters by kind of zone — towns, routes, areas, sites, the Wild and your starter — and the two work together, so "the towns I have not caught in yet" is two clicks. Clicking a Pokémon's sprite opens its summary; one that is dead, released or in the PC says so instead. Your starter is in it, and so is anyone's Gym Leader Challenge type. In a Soul Link it becomes the link's log.

    🎓 Gym Leader Challenge¶

    One elemental type for the whole run. Your random starters are drawn from it, and you may only enter a battle while every Pokémon on your team carries it.

    • Each player is asked at character creation, and answers for themselves. Declining is one click. Server owners can switch the offer off for everyone.
    • An off-type catch still spends the zone. The challenge decides what you may use, not how many tries you get. Off-type Pokémon go to the PC.

    What it builds into the world¶

    • ⚔️ Trainers on the roads¶

      They challenge you on sight and duel each other between fights — but only when it is fair: you are not already battling, they are off cooldown, the levels are close, and you are in front of them. Nothing challenges you from behind. Crouching holds them off, so you can walk up and use the trainer's own menu, and so does riding over a drop with no ground to land on.

      A trainer in a battle cannot be killed, so the fight always resolves. A trainer who has ended a run carries a ☠ tally of them in its name — and so does every other trainer of that name.

    • 🌿 Pokémon on the roads¶

      Every road carries its own encounters: hidden sources set into the paving, each offering one family at its own level. See Route Pokémon.

    • 🧗 The Escape Rope¶

      A one-tap trip back to your respawn point — and it will not fire mid-battle.

    Gyms and the Elite Four

    Since 1.5.58 this mod no longer builds gyms beside towns or an Elite Four on a road: that is the job of PokeGyms and PokeTowns. Gyms already built in an older world stay standing. Wherever a Gym Leader stands, leaders wait to be challenged — you walk in and ask. Lt. Surge is the exception.

    🌿 Route Pokémon¶

    Every generated road carries encounter sources, disguised Cobblemon habitat blocks set into the paving. Each one offers one family of its own: the evolution stage that fits its level, and, when that stage is about to evolve, about one of the next stage for every three, five levels higher.

    • Most sources offer something native to where they stand: the biome on land, the sea or river under a water crossing. A source on a bridge deck leans toward Flying types instead.
    • Each source keeps its own number of Pokémon out, picked once from a range.
    • Now and then a source spawns an alpha, which counts as the zone's encounter like any other.
    • Now and then a source spawns an aggressive Pokémon, which comes after you on sight; its first hit starts a battle.
    • A route too short for the spacing still gets one source, halfway along.

    Needs Cobblemon 1.8. Spacing, levels and odds are all settings — see Route Pokémon settings.

    🧗 Escape Rope¶

    Use it and you are whisked back to your respawn point, the same place a whiteout sends you.
    Where it sends you the last Pokémon Center you used, or your bed or respawn anchor; the world spawn if you have none
    Restrictions Overworld only, never mid-battle, 10-second cooldown
    Cost one rope per use, free in creative

    On a Nuzlocke run, refusing to fire mid-battle is the whole point: a fight you started is a fight you finish.

    🪢 Crafting¶

    A shapeless recipe — the arrangement in the grid does not matter, the ingredients just have to be in the table together. One craft makes two.

    Just want the routes?¶

    The whole ruleset stands down on demand and Routes keeps working — roads, waypoints and map paint all intact. Set Enable Nuzlocke to off on the NUZLOCKE tab at world creation, or run /nuzlocke disable on a world that already exists. To soften one rule instead of all of them, see Commands & Config.
