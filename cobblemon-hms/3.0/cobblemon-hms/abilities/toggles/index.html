    Documented against Cobblemon HMs 3.0.0

    Auras¶

    Auras are passive HMs your Pokémon give you while they are out and near you (24 blocks). Open Cobblemon's interaction wheel on one of your sent-out Pokémon, pick Field moves, and switch an aura on; it shows ✔ while it is on. Recall the Pokémon or walk away from it and the aura stops; it comes back when the Pokémon does.

    You can run as many auras as you like, from one Pokémon or several. They cost nothing.

    Put the Pokémon back in its ball and its auras switch off. Sending it out again does not bring them back: switch them on again from its wheel. Walking away from it only pauses them.

    Weather auras

    Rain Dance, Sunny Day, Blizzard, Sandstorm, Mist and Snowscape are weather. Only one runs at a time: switching one on, on any of your Pokémon, switches the others off, so the last one you switched on is the one in effect. Between players close together, too, the weather switched on last wins. Like any aura, a weather only works while its Pokémon is out and near you, and putting the Pokémon back in its ball switches it off.

    Every weather takes over Minecraft's own sky while it is on: Rain Dance makes it rain, and all the others keep it clear, so no rain falls through your sandstorm, mist or blizzard while their own effects run. Switch it off, or recall the Pokémon, and the game decides the weather again.

    What is on right now

    Open Cobblemon's interaction wheel on any Pokémon: its top-left corner lists every HM in effect, grouped by kind and with its icon, each with the Pokémon doing it in brackets, by its nickname if it has one — for example Sandstorm (Dunes).

    The toggles that make a mount better — Surf, Tailwind, Swift, U-turn, Helping Hand and Agility — belong to the Pokémon you ride: see Your Pokémon.

    Active HMs on your HUD

    The top-left corner of your HUD lists every HM in effect, grouped by kind — Companion (the HM lent to your fist), Aura, Weather and Mount — each with its own icon. Auras no longer show as status effects. Hide the list with Show active HMs on the HUD in the config screen (Mod Menu), or /hms config hud false in singleplayer.

    Who can give it¶

    A Pokémon can give an aura when the move is in its movepool (equipped or not), or when its species is one of these:

    Blizzard (weather) Abomasnow, Glalie, Froslass
    Dive Vaporeon, Lapras, Wailord

    Ability details¶

    Absorb¶

    Power: magnet radius 6

    Loose items on the ground drift toward you while it is on, so a scattered drop or a mined vein gathers itself. Items are nudged rather than yanked, so they keep their normal pickup delay.

    Dive¶

    Lets you live and work underwater. Water stops getting in your way:

    • Breathe underwater — never run out of air.
    • Walk the seabed as if it were dry land — no wading, no drag on every step.
    • Mine at full speed — a block underwater breaks as fast as the same block in open air, and the weight a guardian temple puts on your arms lifts while you're under.

    Dive takes penalties away; it never hands out a bonus. Push off the bottom and you swim at exactly the speed you always did — if you want to be fast in the water, ride a Pokémon with Surf switched on.

    Flash¶

    Lets you see clearly in the dark, even in caves and at night. Toggle off to remove instantly.

    Jump¶

    Power: Jump Boost level (default IV)

    Applies continuous Jump Boost. The power value equals the buff level, so power = 4 gives Jump Boost IV.

    Lava Plume¶

    Power: tenths of the movement multiplier (default 17)

    Dive's twin for lava. It lets you live and work down there: you do not burn, you move through lava and walk its floor without the fluid dragging on every step, and you breathe while you are under. Swimming out is exactly as it always was — like Dive, it takes a penalty away rather than handing out a bonus.

    • Fire: you do not burn while it is on, in lava or out of it.
    • Seeing: with your head under the lava you can see about 24 blocks instead of one, and the floor around you is lit. Wading with your head above the surface leaves the sky as it is.
    • Moving: walking through lava is as quick as walking on land. Sprint and you swim, a little faster, but never faster than you sprint on land.
    • Breathing: your air never runs out while you are under.

    Like every aura it only works while its Pokémon is out and within 24 blocks. If it wanders off, goes back in its ball or you switch the aura off, the fire protection lasts 5 more seconds and you get a warning if you are in lava, enough to climb out.

    It used to need Burning Bulwark for the fire protection. Burning Bulwark went to Cobblemon Action Combat, so Lava Plume now covers that on its own.

    Lucky Chant¶

    Fortune leans your way while it lasts: what you break and what you fish give up a little more.

    Magnet Rise¶

    Power: percent of creative flight speed you get (default 25)

    Flight. Switch it on and double-tap jump to lift off, as in creative mode. You stay up for as long as you like, drifting at a little over half your walking pace, and sprinting gives no dash. While it is on you take no fall damage.

    It is a building tool, not a way to travel: for that, ride one of your flying Pokémon.

    • It ends when the aura does: recall the Pokémon, switch it off, or get more than 24 blocks from it (flying straight up counts). If that happens in the air, you float down on Slow Falling until you land instead of dropping.
    • Logging out while flying is safe: you come back floating down, and switch it on again once your Pokémon is out.
    • It never touches a flight you have from somewhere else (creative mode, another mod, /fly).

    Rock Climb¶

    Every vertical surface behaves like a ladder, and it is steered by the movement keys: W climbs, S descends, and holding nothing holds your place on the wall.

    Your horizontal movement is left alone, so you can travel along a face and step off it whenever you like — a wall is something to climb, not something to be stuck to. On the ground it only takes over when you are actually asking to go up, so walking and jumping near a wall work normally.

    It works on your mount too. Riding a Pokémon, run into a wall holding W and your Pokémon climbs it with you.

    Shadow Sneak¶

    Power: percent of your speed you keep (default 55)

    Total stealth on foot. No footstep sounds, no name over your head, and nothing underfoot notices you — not pressure plates, not tripwires, not a sculk sensor.

    You move slower than a walk for it, though faster than a crouch, so it is something you can actually travel in rather than a stationary hiding trick. You cannot sprint on land while it runs, and climbing a ladder or vine makes no sound either.

    It only works on land: in water or lava you swim at your normal speed, sprint-swimming included.

    If you are already on a scoreboard team

    The hidden nametag is done with a vanilla team, so a player who already belongs to one keeps their team and simply does not get that part of the effect. Everything else still applies.

    Snowscape¶

    Power: trail radius 3

    Winter follows you on foot: snow settles around you as you walk, and still water freezes over as you cross it, the way Frost Walker does. Flowing water is left alone, so a river keeps running instead of being paved, and the ice melts back once you have passed. Where it is too warm to snow (a desert, a jungle, a plain), the snow melts away again shortly behind you; in a cold biome it stays.

    • It works only on foot: not while you ride, swim or fall, so it never freezes the sea under a Pokémon you are surfing on.
    • Only open water freezes. Kelp, seagrass and waterlogged blocks are left as they are, and no ice forms around a creature standing in the water.
    • It changes only what you could change by hand: nothing in adventure mode, in spectator mode or inside spawn protection, and nothing on land a claim mod protects from you.

    Sweet Scent¶

    Cooldown: 60s between arrivals

    Works like a Poké Snack you carry around. While it is on, a wild Pokémon from the area turns up near you about once a minute. It uses Cobblemon's own spawner with the Poké Snack's rules, so what arrives is what lives there, and it never brings a herd.

    The held item is the seasoning. Whatever the Pokémon giving off the scent is holding works the way that item would as a Poké Snack seasoning: a berry that leans a snack toward a type, a nature or a shiny leans the scent the same way. Several Pokémon with Sweet Scent on add their items together, like several seasonings in one snack. The item is not used up. A seasoning that makes a snack bite sooner (a bite-time effect) makes the next arrival come sooner too, with the same chance it has in a snack.

    Arrivals follow the snack's limits: no more than Cobblemon's Poké Snack cap of wild Pokémon per chunk around you, nothing arrives while you are dead or spectating, and the interval never drops below 5 seconds whatever the config says.

    Sandstorm¶

    Power: how many times faster loose ground breaks (4)

    Weather aura, with a clear sky. Sand is your ground while it blows:

    • Soul sand and honey never slow you down: you walk on them as fast as on stone. It works in the Nether too, so a soul sand valley is open ground.
    • Sand, gravel, soul sand, soul soil, clay and concrete powder break 4× as fast, whatever you dig them with. Everything else breaks at its normal speed, so it never makes your pickaxe faster.
    • Another player's weather switched on later, close to you, replaces your sandstorm until you move apart or theirs ends.

    Mist¶

    Power: blocks away a hunter loses you (8)

    Weather aura, with a clear sky. A mist rolls in around you and you can only see about 20 blocks. Anything hunting you loses track of you once you are more than 8 blocks away, and goes back to wandering. Up close it still sees you, so Mist is for slipping away, not for standing in the open.

    In 4.0.0 this aura was called Fog and also came from Haze and Smokescreen. A config file that still says fog is read as Mist.

    Rain Dance¶

    Weather aura. It rains, the game's own rain, for as long as Rain Dance is on and its Pokémon is out. Switch it off or recall the Pokémon and Minecraft takes the weather back.

    Sunny Day¶

    Weather aura. The sky stays clear, with no rain and no storms, for as long as Sunny Day is on and its Pokémon is out. Switch it off or recall the Pokémon and Minecraft takes the weather back.

    Blizzard¶

    Power: radius of the snowfall around you (24)

    Weather aura. Snow falls and settles all around you, whatever the biome, for as long as Blizzard is on and its Pokémon is out. The sky stays clear of vanilla rain meanwhile. The snow is real snow: it piles up to three layers and settles where a real snowfall would, on open ground, never on top of flowers or grass and not right next to a torch.

    Where it is too warm to snow, the snow melts on its own a few minutes after the storm has moved on or been switched off, even across a server restart. Where the biome is cold enough to snow, it is ordinary snow and stays.

    It does not snow in the Nether (there is no sky there, only a roof), and it stops while you are in spectator mode.
